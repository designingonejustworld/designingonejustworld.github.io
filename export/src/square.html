Know your stuff

If you don't understand the issue deeply,
don't speak to the media.

Half knowledge weakens the cause — not just your argument.

Land the message
Your job isn't to
answer everything.
It's to land
your message.

Whatever you're asked, bring it back to the core point.

03
Stay grounded
Don't get defensive.
Don't get emotional.
Stay calm.
Stay factual.

The media decides how you're perceived.

The window
20–30
SECONDS — that's all you've got.

If you can't explain your point clearly and quickly, it won't make it to the story.

@onejust.world

Tip 05 of 08
Receipts beat rhetoric
Facts>Opinions.
Always.
Back every claim with data.
Use government sources wherever possible.
Facts = credibility. Credibility decides coverage.
@onejust.world

Tip 06 of 08
Discipline of the message
One campaign.
One voice.

Mixed messaging across cities = diluted impact.
Stay aligned.

Optional: campaign photo
@onejust.world

Tip 07 of 08
07
Practice = power

Prepare like the media
will challenge you.

(Well, it does get tough!)
Practice with your teammates.
Ask difficult questions.
Play devil's advocate.
@onejust.world

Tip 08 of 08
Hand them the story

Make it EASY for the media to cover you.

01
A 3–4 paragraph press release
Tight. Quotable. No fluff.
02
One strong image
Editorial-ready. High-res.
03
A clear headline
The hook the desk will keep.
04
Spokesperson ready
One line. One name. One number.
They will use what you give them.
